# YA KNOW (The Unspoken Void) — Video Content Treatment
### Music Video Director's Script & Visual Breakdown

**Concept:** The video is a single unbroken journey through one person's mind at the exact moment they try to say something true and can't. No monsters. No gore. The horror is internal. The setting shifts between real-world environments and impossible abstract spaces — always bleeding into each other, never fully one or the other.

**Visual Palette:**
- Colors: Deep black, volcanic red, bioluminescent blue, cathedral gold
- Texture: Film grain over everything — 16mm feel, not clean digital
- Movement: Camera is always *slightly* unstable — handheld when calm, locked-off when chaotic (the inverse of convention — break the viewer's expectations)

---

## SECTION-BY-SECTION VISUAL SCRIPT

---

### [INTRO — THE CRAWL | 48 BPM | 0:00–1:12]

**Location:** An empty cathedral. Stone floor. Stained glass windows that let in no light — the colors bleed inward, not outward.

**Opening shot:** Extreme close-up of a mouth, slightly open. No sound. Lips trembling slightly. Hold for 8 seconds. Viewer doesn't know whose mouth. Doesn't know if it's about to scream or whisper.

**Visual sequence:**
- Pull back *impossibly slowly* — 4 minutes of film condensed into 72 seconds — to reveal a figure sitting in the front pew, alone
- The figure is the **protagonist** — androgynous, dressed in ordinary clothes (not costumed as "metal") — jeans, worn jacket. They look like anyone. They look like you.
- The pipe organ plays — but on screen, no one is playing it. The keys move by themselves.
- The camera circles the protagonist at walking pace, never cutting — one continuous move
- As the 8-string guitar enters (one chord, held), the stained glass windows begin to *crack slowly* from the center outward. Not dramatically. Just: fine lines, like ice breaking.
- The protagonist opens their mouth.

**What happens in the last 10 seconds of the intro:**
The protagonist's mouth opens. Close on the throat. You can see the vocal cords working. You hear nothing but the held guitar chord. Then —

---

### [VERSE I — THE FRACTURE | 192 BPM | 1:12–2:08]

**SMASH CUT.** The cathedral is gone.

**Location:** An industrial space — the protagonist is running. Not from anything visible. Just running. The floor is concrete, wet, reflecting red light from overhead tubes. Handheld camera chases them.

**Visual sequence:**
- The 13/8 time signature becomes the edit rhythm — cuts land on the lurching "wrong" beat, not the obvious one. The viewer feels the time displacement as physical unease.
- Intercut: extreme close-ups during "Ya know" — knuckles whitening around a phone, eyes scanning a face that won't look back, a sentence half-typed then deleted
- The death growl happens in the dark. We never see the vocalist's full face in this section — only their jaw, their neck, their chest. The voice is inhuman but the body is real.
- On "nothing comes, nothing comes, nothing COMES" — the protagonist stops running. Stands still. The camera catches up and PASSES THEM, then circles back. The protagonist is facing us now, mouth open.

**The ONE BEAT SILENCE before the cut:**
Full black. One frame. Then — the Pre-Chorus.

---

### [PRE-CHORUS — THE TENSION | 192 BPM | 2:08–2:35]

**Split screen. Two worlds simultaneously:**

**LEFT PANEL:** The protagonist in a brightly lit, ordinary kitchen. Morning. Coffee brewing. Speaking to someone off-camera. We can't hear what they're saying — the music swallows their words. But we see their face trying to say something real, and failing. They look almost normal. Almost fine.

**RIGHT PANEL:** Abstract space — the protagonist in the same position but surrounded by floating text, fragments of sentences that dissolve before they complete. The clean tenor vocal comes from this panel. The death growl comes from the left — the ordinary world.

**On "Between the thought and the voice / There is a universe of noise":**
The split screen breaks down — the dividing line starts to breathe, expand, contract. The two panels don't stay in their lanes. Kitchen imagery bleeds into the abstract. The abstract bleeds into the kitchen.

**On "all I have is — all I have is —":**
Both panels freeze. Hard cut.

---

### [CHORUS — THE DETONATION | 192 BPM | 2:35–3:30]

**Full screen. The cathedral returns — but wrong.**

The ceilings are gone. Open sky above, but the sky is the wrong color — deep ultraviolet, the color just above black. Stars that aren't stars. The floor is the same stone but the pews are gone. Just the protagonist, alone in the center, surrounded by empty space in every direction.

**Visual approach:**
- The Gregorian choir appears as figures in the distance — dozens of them, robed, approaching in slow motion — but no matter how long the shot holds, they never get closer
- On each "YA KNOW" lyric: a single new light source ignites somewhere in the space — floor, wall, air. By the end of the chorus there are 8 separate light sources, all different colors, none of them illuminating the protagonist directly. They are always just outside the pool of light.
- The protagonist's body reacts physically to the sound — not moshing, not performing — *absorbing* it. Like pressure from all directions. Hair moving in wind that isn't wind.

**On "THE VOID IS JUST A WORD THAT GOT AWAY":**
Every light source goes out simultaneously. Pure black. Three full seconds.

Then: the bioluminescent blue of Verse II seeps in from the edges.

---

### [VERSE II — THE COLLAPSE | 144 BPM | 3:30–4:26]

**Location:** A suburban street. 3AM. The protagonist walks alone. Streetlights. Silence beyond the music.

This verse feels more claustrophobic than the louder sections because of the 5/4 time — there's always one beat extra, and the camera *counts with it*, holding one frame too long at the end of each shot. The viewer feels something is slightly wrong without knowing why.

**Visual sequence:**
- Phone screen close-ups: half-finished texts. Autocomplete suggestions that don't match what they mean. Voice memo app, red recording dot, no audio waveform — just silence being recorded.
- The black metal shriek vocal is visualized as *light distortion* — around the protagonist's head, the air bends slightly, like heat shimmer, but in cold blue
- "Ya know — ya know — ya KNOW (the tongue has no map for the unknown)" — the protagonist stops. Looks up. An overhead shot from directly above: the protagonist is a small figure on an empty street. Tiny. The rest of the frame is asphalt and yellow line.
- "And the knowing eats you alive" — the overhead shot slowly rotates 180 degrees while holding position. The world goes upside down. The protagonist stands on the ceiling of the world, but doesn't fall.

---

### [PRE-CHORUS II | 192 BPM | 4:26–4:55]

**Mirrors Pre-Chorus I — but the images are inverted.**

The kitchen is now the abstract space. The abstract is now what's domestic and familiar. Same split screen, but the panels have switched sides and identities. What was safe is now strange. What was strange now feels like home.

The drums play with brushes here — and the camera reflects this: all the cuts are soft dissolves, not hard cuts. Everything bleeds into everything. Nothing has a clean edge anymore.

**On "YA KNOW —" at the end:** The split screen collapses into one. We're back in the cathedral. The congregation of choir figures is closer now. Still not close enough to touch. Getting closer.

---

### [CHORUS II | 192 BPM | 4:55–5:50]

**Same cathedral. Same visual vocabulary as Chorus I. But:**

The choir figures are inside the cathedral now, filling the pews — and they are singing with their mouths closed. The sound is impossible. It comes from inside them, not from their throats.

The protagonist is in the center again. But now the camera is on the floor looking up, making them enormous. Giant. The pressure of the sound lifts them slightly — barely perceptible — a centimeter off the stone floor.

Hovering.

**On the final "ya know" of the chorus:**
The choir stands. Every figure. Simultaneously. They turn to face the protagonist. We still can't see their faces.

Hard cut to —

---

### [BRIDGE — THE VOID SPEAKS | 72 BPM FREE | 5:50–7:02]

**The most important visual moment of the entire video.**

**Location:** Nowhere. A space that is just light — warm, amber, the color of a candle seen through closed eyelids.

The protagonist is alone. Sitting on the floor of this nowhere space. No instruments visible. Just them and the clean tenor vocal and the (inaudible) cello.

**Visual approach — the three verses of the bridge:**

*"What if ya know is the sound of god trying to explain itself..."*
The camera slowly pushes in on their face. Just their face. For 30 seconds. No cuts. We watch them listen. We watch them start to understand something. Their expression shifts — not to happiness, to recognition.

*"What if ya know is the last two words before the sentence that would change everything stays silent forever"*
The camera is now close enough to see their eyes. The amber light catches the moisture there. They are not crying — they are on the edge of crying. The edge is the whole point. The camera holds.

*"What if every time you've ever said 'ya know' / you were reaching through the dark / and something on the other side — was reaching back"*

On "reaching back" — the camera reverses its entire push-in, pulling back at exactly the speed it came forward. We return to the wide shot. The protagonist is still sitting on the floor.

But now there are two hands visible at the very edge of the frame — reaching toward them from just outside. We don't see who the hands belong to. We don't know if they're reaching toward the protagonist or toward us.

**The cello phrase:** A single animated line traces through the frame. Seven notes drawn in light. It ascends. Does not resolve. Fades.

**Five seconds of silence:** The screen holds on the amber light. Nothing moves. No cut. Let it breathe.

**Then:** From the bottom edge of the frame, a shadow. The 8-string guitar. One chord. The screen begins to darken.

---

### [BREAKDOWN — OBLITERATION | 160→192 BPM | 7:02–8:00]

**The video goes fully abstract here.**

No recognizable location. No human figure. Pure visual music.

**Approach:**
- Oscilloscope-style waveform visualizations — not polished, not clean — printed on 16mm, jittery
- The "3 against 4 against 5" polyrhythm is shown literally: three shapes, four colors, five movement speeds, all overlaid. None aligning. The composition is calculated chaos.
- The syllables "YUH — KNOW" appear as typography — shattered into individual letters, the letters into strokes, the strokes into points — reassembling into new configurations that never stay long enough to read
- As the tempo accelerates to 192: the visuals accelerate with it. Frame rate drops (more motion blur, less resolution) while tempo climbs. The image degrades as the speed increases.

**On the final isolated "KNOW" and the singular unified HIT:**
A single pure white frame. Full screen. Maximum brightness. One frame. Then —

Two seconds of black.

---

### [GUITAR SOLO — THE ORACLE | 192 BPM NO TIME SIG | 8:00–8:52]

**Four sections. Four visual movements. No cuts between them — all done in camera.**

**SECTION A — "THE QUESTION":**
Return to the cathedral, lit in cathedral gold. The guitarist visible for the first time — we see their hands only. The notes they play appear as particles of light that drift upward and dissolve. Clean, precise, beautiful.

**SECTION B — "THE FRACTURE":**
The light particles begin colliding instead of dissolving. The whammy bar divebombs = the camera lurches downward and catches itself. The cathedral walls crack the same way the stained glass did in the intro — same pattern, scaling up. The hands on the guitar lose their calm precision. They become frantic.

**SECTION C — "THE SEARCH":**
Two-handed tapping on the guitar — and the camera begins moving in two independent directions simultaneously (this is a visual effect, not physically possible, but achievable in post). Two conversations. Searching. The guitar and the camera are both looking for something.

**SECTION D — "THE ANSWER":**
Single notes. The camera settles. Completely still — locked off for the first time since the intro. The gold light softens to amber — the same amber as the Bridge.

The protagonist walks into frame from the left. They stand in front of the guitarist. We still only see the guitarist's hands. But the protagonist is watching. And on the three long bent notes at the end — the protagonist nods.

Not dramatically. Just: a nod.

Fade into the opening riff of the Final Chorus.

---

### [FINAL CHORUS — RESURRECTION | 192 BPM | 8:52–9:35]

**Same cathedral. Same everything. But:**

The choir figures are finally close enough to see — and they have faces. They are different people. Every age. Every kind of person. They look like the people you know. They look like anyone. They look like you.

And they are singing — mouths open now — and for the first time in the video we can *see* the sound. It comes out of them as light. Gold. Warm. Not fire — warmer than fire. It rises.

**The three vocal layers unified:**
The three vocalists are visible for the first time, standing together in the center of the cathedral — clean tenor, death growl vocalist, black metal shriek vocalist — and they are facing each other instead of the camera. They are singing to each other, not at us.

The camera moves slowly overhead — rising — until we're looking down at the entire cathedral from above. The congregation. The vocalists. The protagonist standing among them.

**On the stretched-out final lines:**
"YA KNOW — YA KNOW — THE VOID IS JUST A WORD — THAT GOT AWAY — YA — KNOW —"

The camera keeps rising. The cathedral shrinks. We pull back through the roof, through the sky, up — until the cathedral is a point of gold light among darkness.

Then: the 8-string guitar. One chord. Same as the first.

**The screen holds on that point of light for a long moment. Then the light goes out.**

---

### [OUTRO — THE ECHO | 48 BPM | 9:35–9:47]

**Wide shot: dawn. A city. Ordinary. People beginning their day.**

The protagonist is on a street corner, watching. They have their phone out. They are composing a message. We do not see who it's to. We watch them type and delete. Type and delete. Type and delete.

Then they stop deleting. They keep what they wrote. We don't see what it says.

They hit send.

Cut to black.

**The voice, dry, no reverb, speaking in the silence:**
*"Ya know."*

**Hold black for three full seconds. Let the viewer sit in it.**

**END.**

## PRODUCTION NOTES FOR DIRECTOR / DP

**Casting:** The protagonist should not read as "a heavy metal fan." Intentionally ordinary. The more ordinary, the better. The point is that this internal experience — the gap between what you feel and what you can say — belongs to everyone.

**Camera:** ARRI Alexa with anamorphic lenses for the real-world sections. Probe lens for extreme close-ups on mouths, eyes, hands. Abstract sections can use any approach — this is where the DP has full latitude.

**Color grading:**
- Real world: slightly desaturated, true-to-life — familiar, domestic, almost boring
- Cathedral: cathedral gold bleeding into ultraviolet
- Abstract sections: no rules — each section gets its own palette, but always pushing toward the dark end

**Editing:** The edit should track the time signature changes, not the obvious beat. Cuts on the 7th beat in 7/8. Cuts landing on the unexpected 13th beat in 13/8. This will take a meticulous edit and the editor needs the grid explained — or ideally is a musician.

**The silence moments:** Print them to picture. No fade, no breathe. Digital zero. Force the edit to honor the rests.

**Runtime:** 9 minutes 47 seconds. Do not cut it short. This song earns its length.
